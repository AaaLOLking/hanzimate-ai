# Pedagogy

## Mission first

Tie each activity to a concrete real-world outcome. A useful Mission states:

- why the learner needs Chinese;
- what success looks like;
- deadline and available time;
- constraints and preferences;
- adjacent topics that are currently out of scope.

Do not change a Mission without learner confirmation.

## Zone of proximal development

Choose a task the learner can complete with limited support. Reduce difficulty when the learner cannot start; increase it when answers are automatic and error-free.

Useful difficulty controls:

- vocabulary frequency and HSK band;
- sentence length;
- speech speed;
- number of distractors;
- visual, pinyin, translation, or example support;
- time pressure;
- familiarity of scenario;
- amount of output required.

Change one or two controls at a time so the source of difficulty remains interpretable.

## Fluency and storage

Fluency is successful performance now. Storage is successful retrieval later. Record exposure separately from demonstrated learning.

Build storage through:

- retrieval before re-reading;
- spacing across days;
- interleaving related skills after each is introduced;
- varied examples that preserve the same rule;
- delayed use in a realistic task.

## Lesson shape

1. State one observable objective.
2. Activate one relevant prior idea.
3. Explain only what is necessary.
4. Model two contrasting examples.
5. Ask for guided production.
6. Give immediate feedback.
7. Ask for unaided retrieval.
8. End with a real-life transfer task and next review.

Aim for 8–15 minutes. Split a lesson if it needs more than one major grammatical idea.

## Feedback

Use a tight loop:

- identify what worked;
- show the highest-value change;
- explain briefly;
- ask the learner to try again;
- confirm whether the new attempt demonstrates the target.

Do not rewrite every sentence into native-level prose. Preserve the learner's intended meaning and voice.

## Support language

Use Chinese that is comprehensible at the learner's level. Use the support language for safety, task instructions, or a short explanation when Chinese would consume working memory needed for practice.

Pinyin is a scaffold, not a permanent default. Hide it once the learner can retrieve the characters and pronunciation reliably.

## Evidence

Evidence is stronger in this order:

1. delayed unaided production;
2. immediate unaided production;
3. recognition among plausible options;
4. self-report;
5. content merely viewed.

Only the first three can directly support mastery estimates.
